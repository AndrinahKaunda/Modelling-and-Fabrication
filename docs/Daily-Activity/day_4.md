Grain & Fiber Direction
For materials like wood, the grain and fiber
direction are critical. They determine how the

break.

Orientation in Fabrication
How a material is oriented during cutting, forming, or assembly can dramatically affect the final
product's performance and durability.


!!! note:
Choose materials based on performance requirements, not just aesthetic
appeal

-Wood was considered for its natural strength and suitability for laser cutting and CNC routing, with attention to grain direction.

-Plastics were selected for lightweight applications but required careful heat management during fabrication.

-Metals were identified for high-strength applications despite higher cost and energy requirements.

-Composites were evaluated for high performance where strength-to-weight ratio was critical.

### Fabrication Approaches: Subtractive vs. Additive
    
#### Subtractive Manufacturing
    • Removes material from a block
    • Examples: CNC milling, laser cutting
    • Excels in precision and surface finish

### Additive Manufacturing
    • Builds objects layer by layer
    • Example: 3D printing
    • Ideal for complexity and customization

Materials are never neutral; they actively shape every aspect of a
design. From the initial concept, materials dictate form, influence

They dictate the possible forms your design can take.
They determine the inherent structure and strength.
They guide the fabrication logic and assembly process.

### CONSTRUCTION METHODS
Assembly as a Fabrication Method
Assembly isn't just the final step; it's often the core fabrication method itself. Well-designed
joints can reduce manufacturing complexity and material waste.
Versatile Joining Techniques
Many designs rely on press-fit joints, screws, fasteners, and adhesives. These methods
offer flexibility and adaptability across various materials.
Efficiency & Sustainability
Strategically using assembly techniques can significantly reduce machine time and
material waste, leading to more sustainable and cost-effective production.


ADVANCED APPROACHES
Hybrid Fabrication Methods
1
Combining Processes
Integrating diverse methods like CNC machining with 3D printing, or laser cutting with material forming, unlocks new possibilities.
2
Enhanced Performance
Hybrid approaches can yield products with superior performance characteristics that no single method could achieve alone.
3
Accelerated Prototyping
By leveraging the strengths of different machines, designers can accelerate prototyping cycles, refining ideas more quickly.
4
Cross-Process Planning
Successful hybrid fabrication demands meticulous planning and understanding of how each process interacts with the materials.

### DESIGN

Precision vs. Expressiveness
Digital fabrication tools offer unparalleled precision, capable of achieving tight tolerances. However, real-world materials inherently introduce variation.
The Designer's Choice:
Tight Tolerances: Pursuing absolute accuracy for critical functional
components.
Expressive Imperfections: Embracing natural variations and
crafting them into the aesthetic and character of the design.

## Reflections/ Outcome
Understanding how materials react to external forces during fabrication
is crucial for predictable outcomes and quality control.
The main outcome was developing a material-first mindset, where design decisions are informed by material behavior and fabrication constraints. This approach leads to more efficient, sustainable, and reliable designs while reducing fabrication errors and material waste.

Good design balances performance, Cost, Durability & Lifecycle: Good design balances performance, cost, and responsibility. It considers material price, tooling, fabrication time, and the product's entire lifecycle from use to end-of-life. Can the design do more with less material? This question drives innovation in sustainable design.
